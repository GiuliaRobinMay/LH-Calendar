# The seasons behind US assistance — research notes

Compiled 7 September 2026, extended 8 September with a sweep of which grant
domains are genuinely seasonal. For the Lesko Help calendar.

Two questions were on the table: **is there a real seasonal structure worth
building a calendar around**, and **has anyone already built it well**.

Short answers: yes, and no.

---

## 1. What the seasons actually are

The pattern that matters is not that programmes have deadlines. It is that the
*published* deadline and the *real* deadline are usually different dates, and
the gap between them is where people lose money.

Four distinct shapes turned up, and the calendar's data model is built around
them rather than around months.

### Shape A — Fixed national window

Same dates everywhere, published a year in advance, genuinely closed outside
them. Rare, but these are the biggest.

| Programme | Window | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Medicare Open Enrollment | **15 Oct – 7 Dec 2026** | Coverage starts 1 Jan. Same dates every year. |
| Marketplace health insurance (ACA) | **1 Nov 2026 – 15 Jan 2027** | CMS confirmed these dates in early August 2026. 15 Dec is the cutoff for coverage starting 1 Jan. A few state-run exchanges run to 31 Jan. |
| Medicare Advantage second chance | **1 Jan – 31 Mar 2027** | Only for people already in an MA plan. |
| Tax filing | **late Jan – 15 Apr 2027** | IRS names the exact opening day each January; late January is the settled pattern. |

One legal date deserves its own row because it is widely misunderstood: refunds
containing the **Earned Income Credit or Additional Child Tax Credit cannot be
issued before mid-February** — 15 February in practice. This applies however you
file, including at a free VITA site. People plan around a January refund that
cannot legally arrive.

### Shape B — First-come, first-served until the money is gone

The published closing date is fiction. The real deadline is the day funding is
exhausted, which is frequently months earlier. **This is the single most
valuable thing a calendar can communicate**, and no competitor does it.

- **LIHEAP (heating bill help)** is the clearest case. Most states open 1 October
  or 1 November and publish a spring closing date, but the money runs out first
  in much of the country. Confirmed variation for the 2026–27 season:
  Massachusetts opens 1 Oct; Illinois opens 1 Oct for seniors, people with a
  disability, families with children under five, and anyone disconnected, then
  1 Nov for everyone else; Pennsylvania opens 2 Nov and expects to close
  23 Apr 2027.
- **Weatherization** has no deadline at all and a waiting list measured in
  months or years, which makes "apply early" the entire strategy.
- **State and college financial aid** behaves this way even though FAFSA itself
  does not — see below.

### Shape C — Genuinely local, genuinely variable

Asserting a national date here would be actively harmful. The calendar marks
these `varies` and says "check yours" on the front of the card rather than in a
footnote.

- **Property tax relief / homestead exemptions** — county-level deadlines
  spread across the whole year. Many people qualify for years without filing
  because nobody tells them.
- **Head Start** — spring enrolment for a fall start is the pattern, but seats
  reopen all year as families move.
- **LIHEAP cooling** — the widest variation of anything researched. Some states
  run no cooling programme at all.
- **Holiday assistance** (Angel Tree, Toys for Tots) — each local office sets
  its own dates. Some open 1 Sep, most open 1 Oct, and a great many accept
  nothing after October. Asking in December is two months late, which is
  exactly when people ask.

### Shape D — Unpredictable, opens without warning, closes fast

Nothing on a calendar can predict these, so the calendar's job changes: get the
paperwork ready *before* the window exists.

- **Section 8 / Housing Choice Voucher waiting lists** — a housing authority may
  open its list for a few days, sometimes years apart. Some close in 48 hours.
- **FEMA Individual Assistance** — opens only on a federal disaster declaration,
  then runs roughly 60 days. The clock starts at the declaration, not the storm,
  and people burn the first month waiting on an insurance decision.

### The FAFSA trap, which deserves its own note

The 2027–28 FAFSA opens on or about **1 October 2026** — the statutory date,
though the last two cycles opened slightly early (2026–27 opened 24 September
2025). Its federal deadline is **30 June 2028**, nearly two years out.

That federal deadline is the trap. The money that actually runs out is state and
college aid, much of it first-come first-served with priority deadlines between
December and March. A calendar that displays only the federal date tells people
they have two years when they effectively have ninety days. The calendar
therefore carries both cycles as separate cards, with the priority season marked
as its own key date.

### Programmes with no season at all

Worth stating loudly, because "is it too late?" stops many people from applying:
**Medicaid, CHIP, SNAP and WIC accept applications every day of the year.**
For these the risk is the opposite one — silent lapse at recertification. Most
people who lose Medicaid lose it for missing a renewal letter, not for earning
too much. The calendar surfaces renewal as a first-class field.

## 1b. Which domains are actually seasonal

The calendar only carries domains where timing can cost you the money. This
sweep was to decide what earns a place and what does not.

**Seasonal — these are on the calendar**

| Domain | Why it qualifies |
|---|---|
| Health coverage enrolment | The hardest deadlines in the whole set. Marketplace 1 Nov – 15 Jan, Medicare 15 Oct – 7 Dec, Medicare Advantage 1 Jan – 31 Mar. Fixed in law, identical nationwide, and missing one costs a full year. |
| Energy & utility bills | LIHEAP opens Oct/Nov and closes on funding, not on the published date. Winter shut-off moratoriums are their own seasonal window — typically 15 Nov – 31 Mar. |
| Student aid & scholarships | FAFSA opens ~1 Oct; state and college money runs out Dec–Mar. National scholarships Aug–Dec, local Jan–Apr. |
| Tax credits & money back | One filing window, late Jan – 15 Apr, plus the 15 Feb EITC/ACTC refund hold and the 15 Oct extension cut-off. |
| Food & the school year | Summer EBT closes 31 Aug; school meal applications open with the school year. |
| Seasonal charity | Holiday assistance signs up Sep–Oct for December. Back-to-school drives run late Jul–Aug. Both are over before most people think to ask. |
| Childcare & early years | Head Start enrols in spring for an autumn start. |
| Farm & land | USDA set a national **15 January** batching deadline for the first EQIP, CSP and ACEP funding round. FSA safety-net and crop insurance sales closing dates are hard deadlines with no late filing. |
| Arts & culture | NEA Grants for Arts Projects runs exactly two cycles a year (FY2026: 12 Feb and 9 Jul), and the application is two-part — part one on Grants.gov, part two on the NEA portal ~12 days later. Miss part one and the system will not let you file part two at all. ~40% of the NEA budget passes to state arts agencies, which run their own cycles. |

**Not seasonal — deliberately excluded from the calendar**

SNAP, WIC, Medicaid and CHIP all take applications every day of the year with
no enrolment window; the risk there is silent lapse at recertification, not a
missed opening. Weatherization is rolling with a long waiting list. Section 8
waiting lists are episodic rather than seasonal — unpredictable, sometimes
years apart, open for as little as 48 hours. FEMA Individual Assistance is
event-driven: it opens on a federal disaster declaration and then runs about 60
days. All of these sit in a separate list beneath the calendar, where they do
not imply a deadline that does not exist.

**A correction worth recording.** The intuition that health coverage and home
energy are "always available" is exactly backwards. They are the two most
rigidly seasonal domains in American assistance, and they are the main reason a
calendar is worth building at all. What *is* always available is food and
medical assistance — SNAP, WIC, Medicaid — which is the opposite pairing.

**Property tax relief** sits awkwardly and is marked `varies`: around 29 states
plus DC run an income-based circuit breaker and at least 26 offer a senior
freeze, but deadlines are county-level. Most fall Mar–Jun, some as early as
February, and Indiana wants the over-65 deduction by 31 December of the prior
year.

## 2. What the competition is doing

### Nonprofit-facing grant tools — Instrumentl, Candid, GrantStation

Mature, capable, and aimed at somebody else entirely. These are pipeline
managers for grant writers: deadline trackers, reporting calendars, funder
matching, internal-vs-external deadline fields, subscription-priced. Instrumentl
offers calendar and tracker views; GrantStation pushes reminders by newsletter.

Not competitors. They serve the organisation applying for a $50,000 foundation
grant, not the household trying to work out whether it is too late to get the
heating bill covered.

### Government sources — Grants.gov, Benefits.gov, healthcare.gov, medicare.gov

Grants.gov is filterable by close date but is a federal funding-opportunity
database, not consumer-facing. Benefits.gov answers *whether you qualify*, never
*when to apply*. The programme sites each show their own date prominently and
nobody else's — which is correct for them and useless as a planning tool, since
no household's needs sit inside one agency.

### Consumer benefit apps — Propel, findhelp, mRelief

Propel (5m+ users) is the strongest product in the space, but its calendar is
about **money arriving**: EBT balance, deposit dates, benefit news. findhelp and
mRelief are eligibility screeners and referral directories. All three answer
"what am I entitled to" and "did my money land". None answers "what closes this
month".

### The closest things that exist

Two were found, and both are articles rather than products:

- **Uplift** publishes a benefits enrollment calendar page.
- **BenefitsUSA** runs blog posts covering when to apply, LIHEAP deadlines by
  state, and a renewal calendar.

Both are static prose, organised by programme, undated relative to the reader,
and not filterable. Neither tells you what is open *today*.

Scholarship sites (Fastweb, Scholarships.com and the many month-by-month
listicles) do publish deadline calendars, but only for scholarships. The useful
finding from that corner: national awards open August–September and close
October–December, while local awards — community foundation, employer, union,
school — open in January and close March–April, and have dramatically fewer
applicants. Best odds on the whole calendar, and almost nobody works them.

### The gap

Nobody is running a **consumer-facing, cross-programme, status-first** view. The
existing work is either the wrong audience (grant-writer SaaS), the wrong
question (eligibility screeners), the wrong scope (one agency), or the wrong
format (an article you have to read and date-check yourself).

That gap is the product, and it is narrow enough to be defensible:

1. **Status before date.** The first thing shown is *open / closing / opens
   soon*, computed against today, not a list of dates to interpret.
2. **Honest about certainty.** Dates that vary by state are drawn differently
   and labelled on the card face. A confident wrong date costs somebody a
   benefit; competitors assert national dates for state programmes routinely.
3. **The real deadline, not the published one.** "Apply the week it opens, not
   the month it closes" is the actual advice for first-come programmes, and it
   is nowhere in the existing material.
4. **Prep lead time.** Telling somebody a window opens in three weeks is only
   useful alongside what to gather in those three weeks.

## 4. What this v1 deliberately does not do

Flagged so they are decisions rather than omissions.

- **No state filter.** The honest answer for a dozen programmes is "it depends
  on your state", and the right fix is per-state data, not a dropdown over
  national data. That is the obvious v2, and the data model already carries a
  `precision` field to hang it on.
- **No reminders.** The highest-value feature here is almost certainly an alert
  three weeks before a window opens. It needs somewhere to store a member's
  list, which means wiring into the community platform rather than a static page.
- **No link from the quiz.** The quiz already knows which of the four suits a
  member checked. Landing them on a calendar pre-filtered to those topics is a
  small change and probably the strongest single follow-up.
- **Roughly 22 programmes, not 200.** Every entry was checked by hand. Padding
  the list with unverified dates would undo the one thing that makes this
  better than the articles.
